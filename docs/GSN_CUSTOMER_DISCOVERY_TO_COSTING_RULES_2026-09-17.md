# GSN Customer Discovery To Costing Rules

Date: 2026-09-17
Status: Draft operating rule
Product name: GSN

## Purpose

This document describes how GSN customer discovery should feed directly into pricing. The goal is that after a discovery interview, GSN should be able to produce a sensible price for that organisation without guessing.

## Core Principle

Pricing should not begin with a flat subscription. Pricing should begin with discovery evidence:

- who the organisation is
- where it operates
- what it wants GSN to solve
- how many members or users it has
- what features it will consume
- who will pay
- whether it can generate value for itself, its members, GSN, or sponsors

## Discovery Inputs Required

| Discovery input | Why it matters for costing |
| --- | --- |
| Organisation type | Schools, churches, universities, charities, commercial bodies, and informal groups have different ability and willingness to pay. |
| Region/country | UK/Europe/US pricing cannot simply be copied into lower-income regions. |
| Member/user count | More members create more traffic, support, storage, and operational value. |
| Active usage expectation | A large inactive community should not cost the same as a smaller highly active one. |
| Required features | Community Domain, Shop Diaries, VAULT, verification, DemandBox, finance, trust tools, and analytics create different cost and value. |
| Revenue ability | A commercial or fee-collecting body should usually pay more than a poor community organisation. |
| Payment source | Self-paid, sponsor-paid, donor-paid, grant-paid, member-paid, or hybrid payment routes change the quote and reporting duties. |
| Payment term | Monthly, annual, multi-year, and direct debit commitments affect risk and discount. |
| Sponsor value | Some communities may not pay directly but may justify donor, charity, NGO, or grant support. |
| Evidence value to GSN | Pilot learning, testimonials, case studies, adoption data, and community outcomes may partly offset low direct payment. |

## Pricing Output

After discovery, GSN should produce:

1. Recommended package.
2. Monthly price.
3. Annual price.
4. What is included.
5. What is paid separately.
6. Who pays.
7. Sponsor or grant route, if needed.
8. Expected review date.
9. Growth trigger, such as member-count or usage increase.
10. Notes explaining why the quote is fair.

## Example Logic

| Scenario | Likely outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Small poor community with high social value | Very low or free base access, sponsor route opened, member-paid or donor-paid verification credits. |
| Successful church in UK/Europe/US | Paid Community Domain subscription, member-paid verification where suitable, paid extra capacity as needed. |
| Commercial faith organisation in Africa | Treat closer to commercial/community hybrid; do not assume charity pricing. |
| University or successful secondary school | Affordable institutional subscription, likely around a lower professional band, with paid extras. |
| Large organisation using many features | Higher subscription based on members, traffic, governance needs, and support burden. |
| Sponsor-funded NGO community | Sponsor pays base package and agreed credits; GSN provides reporting on usage and outcomes. |

## Demand And Activity Reporting Link

Demand activity should become part of costing and value reporting. GSN should be able to report, by community and time period:

- demands introduced
- demands attended to
- demands resolved
- demands ignored or expired
- offers made in response
- members or organisations participating
- value generated or opportunity created

This matters because a community that cannot pay cash may still prove value through activity, evidence, and outcomes that attract sponsors.

## Next Implementation Direction

The customer discovery form should eventually become a pricing calculator. Assisted onboarding and self-service onboarding should ask the same essential questions, then produce a quote using the same internal costing rules.
