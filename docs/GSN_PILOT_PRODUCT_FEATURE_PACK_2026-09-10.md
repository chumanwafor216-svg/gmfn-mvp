# GSN Pilot Product Feature Pack

Date: 2026-09-10
Status: public-facing working draft
Owner: Global Support Network Ltd

## Purpose

This pack explains the main GSN products that a community can test during a
pilot. It is written for community leaders, secretaries, administrators,
pastors, school/society officers, marketplace organisers, charities and diaspora
associations.

It explains what each product does and how it can help in real life. It does
not explain GSN's internal architecture, trust formulas, database logic,
security workflows, or technical implementation.

## Short Description

Global Support Network Ltd is building GSN, digital infrastructure for organised
communities.

GSN helps communities identify members, communicate officially, track promises,
preserve meeting evidence, organise requests for help, make member businesses
visible, support contribution discipline, and reduce the loss of value that
happens when everything is scattered across WhatsApp, paper notes, emails,
spreadsheets and memory.

GSN is not intended to replace human leadership or ordinary community
conversation. It helps preserve the important operational layer so people can
see what was agreed, what is due, who responded, what remains unresolved, and
what evidence exists.

## Pilot Product List

### 1. GSN Online Member ID Card And Instant Verification

What it is:

A digital member identity card and verification route for people connected to a
GSN community.

What it helps with:

- confirming that someone is recognised in a community;
- showing membership/currentness quickly;
- reducing confusion where many people belong to a group;
- supporting controlled access to community opportunities, services or records.

Real-life use:

A church, school, marketplace or association can verify a member before adding
them to a duty list, giving them access to a community space, checking
attendance, or confirming their community connection.

Pilot value:

The community can test whether members and admins understand the card and
whether instant verification reduces uncertainty.

Boundary:

This verifies GSN-held membership or identity status. It does not replace
passport checks, DBS checks, right-to-work checks, safeguarding checks, bank KYC
or government ID.

### 2. GSN TrustSlip

What it is:

A portable trust-evidence summary that a member can use to show selected current
community evidence.

What it helps with:

- showing a member's participation or reliability signals;
- helping a person avoid starting from zero in every new setting;
- giving controlled evidence before another person takes a risk;
- supporting trade, work, housing, support, volunteering or community decisions.

Real-life use:

A member who has participated, repaid, volunteered, supported others, attended
meetings, or completed commitments can show a TrustSlip instead of relying only
on verbal claims.

Pilot value:

A pilot community can test whether controlled trust evidence helps members gain
confidence, support or opportunity.

Boundary:

A TrustSlip is not a guarantee of future behaviour and is not legal proof by
itself. It is a structured evidence aid.

### 3. GSN Trust Passport

What it is:

A broader trust profile that helps a member understand and build their community
record over time.

What it helps with:

- preserving a fuller participation and trust story;
- linking community activity, support, repayment, service, membership and
  evidence signals;
- helping a member or organisation see what strengthens or weakens trust.

Real-life use:

A member who is active across different communities can build a wider record of
useful participation, contribution and reliability.

Pilot value:

The community can test whether a fuller trust profile encourages better
participation and responsible behaviour.

Boundary:

Trust Passport should guide understanding. It must not become a black-box
judgement, punishment system or uncontrolled public exposure tool.

### 4. GSN Community Bulletin And Committee Bulletin

What it is:

An official notice and committee communication product for community updates,
meetings, attendance, acknowledgements and follow-up.

What it helps with:

- separating official information from casual chat;
- showing who has seen or acknowledged a notice;
- asking who is coming, not coming or unsure;
- helping admins follow up with non-responders;
- reducing missed opportunities when members belong to more than one community.

Real-life use:

A secretary posts a meeting notice or event announcement. GSN helps show who
acknowledged it, who is attending, and who still needs follow-up.

Pilot value:

The community can test whether official notices become easier to manage than
WhatsApp-only announcements.

Boundary:

The Bulletin does not replace all discussion. It organises official information
and follow-up.

### 5. GSN Focus Commitments

What it is:

A promise-to-proof tracker for commitments, deadlines, volunteers, payments,
deliveries, follow-ups, projects and attendance.

What it helps with:

- recording who promised what;
- tracking when the promise is due;
- flagging what is green, yellow or red;
- keeping outside links to WhatsApp, spreadsheets or other tools;
- reducing the secretary burden when many people promise to help.

Real-life use:

For an end-of-year event, many people may promise food, money, transport,
equipment or volunteer time. GSN keeps the commitment list clear while WhatsApp
remains the conversation space.

Pilot value:

The community can test whether promises become easier to follow up and whether
fewer commitments disappear into chat.

Boundary:

Focus Commitments is not a full CRM, legal contract, payment processor or
automatic WhatsApp reader.

### 6. GSN Meeting Pack And Monthly Management Pack

What it is:

A meeting and management summary product that helps a community prepare,
summarise, preserve and follow up meetings.

What it helps with:

- monthly management meeting preparation;
- meeting summaries;
- action lists and responsible people;
- attendance and acknowledgement records;
- unresolved issues and follow-up needs;
- continuity when leaders, secretaries or committees change;
- evidence for grants, sponsors, funders or partners.

Real-life use:

A charity, church, school society, marketplace association or diaspora group can
summarise what was discussed, what was decided, what remains unresolved and what
evidence exists without searching through many scattered places.

Pilot value:

This may be one of the strongest pilot products because almost every organised
community has meetings and continuity problems.

Boundary:

A Meeting Pack is not a full audio/video meeting recorder and does not capture every spoken word by itself. It preserves the important meeting summary, decisions, actions, attendance/acknowledgement, unresolved items and continuity record. It is not automatically a legal board minute unless the organisation formally adopts it under its own governance rules.

### 7. GSN Demand Box And Support Tracker

What it is:

A structured place for needs, requests, welfare issues, community support needs,
service requests and demand signals.

What it helps with:

- seeing who needs help;
- seeing who has responded;
- tracking what remains unresolved;
- recording why a request has not yet been solved;
- understanding repeated community needs;
- supporting grant or sponsor evidence where appropriate.

Real-life use:

A family may need support but have no business, service or product to offer.
Instead of the matter being lost in chat, the committee can see the request,
responses, unresolved status and follow-up requirement.

Pilot value:

The community can test whether help requests become more organised, less
random, and easier for leaders to review.

Boundary:

Sensitive hardship, welfare, family, health, immigration or safeguarding
information must be handled carefully. GSN should show enough for action, not
expose private hardship unnecessarily.

### 8. GSN Community Records And Evidence Pack

What it is:

A structured record layer for community actions, participation, confirmations,
outcomes and evidence.

What it helps with:

- preserving what the community has done;
- making volunteer and support activity visible;
- preparing evidence for sponsors, funders or future partners;
- reducing dependence on one secretary's memory;
- supporting leadership transition.

Real-life use:

A community can show records of meetings, support actions, participation,
verified members, notices, responses, services, commitments and outcomes.

Pilot value:

The community can test whether GSN helps turn ordinary community work into a
clearer evidence trail.

Boundary:

Community Records are evidence aids. Their legal, audit or governance value
depends on how the organisation formally uses and approves them.

### 9. GSN Marketplace And Shop

What it is:

A trusted community marketplace and member business visibility product.

What it helps with:

- making member goods and services easier to find;
- helping buyers discover trusted sellers;
- giving members a visible shop or service page;
- reducing hidden talent and lost opportunity;
- connecting demand with supply.

Real-life use:

A church, school, marketplace or diaspora association may have members who cook,
trade, tutor, repair, transport, consult, clean, design or sell goods. GSN helps
make that value visible.

Pilot value:

The community can test whether member businesses and services receive clearer
visibility and whether buyers feel more confident.

Boundary:

GSN does not automatically guarantee product quality, payment completion,
consumer-law compliance or professional licensing.

### 10. GSN Spotlight And Network Repost

What it is:

A visibility product for announcements, products, services, campaigns, member
businesses and opportunities that need attention.

What it helps with:

- giving selected items stronger visibility;
- helping opportunities travel beyond one quiet corner;
- supporting events, campaigns, shops, services or community needs;
- making time-sensitive opportunities harder to miss.

Real-life use:

A member launches a service, a school society promotes an event, a marketplace
highlights a seller, or a charity promotes a support campaign. Spotlight gives
it controlled attention. Repost can extend reach where allowed.

Pilot value:

The community can test whether organised visibility is more effective than
posting repeatedly in chat.

Boundary:

Spotlight and Repost must not become spam or uncontrolled cross-community
exposure. Visibility needs permission and governance.

### 11. GSN ROSCA And Contribution Responsibilities

What it is:

A contribution-responsibility and rotating-support visibility product for
communities that organise money or mutual-help contributions.

What it helps with:

- seeing contribution responsibilities;
- tracking what is due or outstanding;
- connecting financial discipline with trust evidence;
- helping community value support members, families and small businesses;
- reducing waste where money is gathered but outcomes are unclear.

Real-life use:

A diaspora association or marketplace group may gather contributions. GSN can
help show responsibilities, due dates, repayment/follow-up signals and evidence
of contribution behaviour.

Pilot value:

The community can test whether contribution responsibilities become clearer and
whether money-related trust improves.

Boundary:

This area must be handled carefully. GSN is not a bank, deposit-taker,
regulated lender, insurer or financial adviser. Any real lending, saving,
guarantee or money-handling model needs legal, financial and governance review.

### 12. GSN Community Domain

What it is:

A more governed GSN operating space for formal organisations or organised
community structures.

What it helps with:

- giving an institution a protected community identity;
- connecting membership, notices, records, marketplace, support and governance;
- helping formal bodies manage community activity more responsibly;
- supporting churches, schools, associations, charities and marketplaces.

Real-life use:

A church, school, charity, association or marketplace can use Community Domain
as a more official GSN space rather than operating only as an informal group.

Pilot value:

The organisation can test whether a governed GSN space improves structure,
continuity, records and member trust.

Boundary:

Community Domain does not make GSN the legal owner, regulator, accountant,
trustee or safeguarding authority of the organisation. The organisation remains
responsible for its own governance.

## Four Pilot Stories

### Church Story

A church secretary is planning a large event. Many members promise food, money,
transport and volunteer time. In WhatsApp, the list becomes crowded and hard to
follow. With GSN, the church keeps WhatsApp for normal conversation but uses the
Bulletin for official notices, Focus Commitments for promises, Meeting Pack for
committee summaries, Demand Box for welfare/support needs, and TrustSlip/ID Card
for member recognition and evidence.

### School Or College Story

A student society changes committee every year. Without structure, meeting
notes, project evidence, attendance and responsibilities disappear when leaders
graduate. With GSN, the society keeps member identity, meeting packs,
commitments, notices, project evidence and participation records in one
continuity system.

### Marketplace Story

A marketplace group has traders, buyers, services and needs, but visibility is
random. Some people shout loudly in chat while quieter sellers are missed. With
GSN, sellers can have shops, buyers can post demand, trusted activity can build
evidence, and selected products or services can receive Spotlight or Repost
visibility.

### Diaspora Association Story

A diaspora association supports members, organises events, collects
contributions, promotes businesses and responds to hardship. Without structure,
value is lost in WhatsApp and committee memory. With GSN, the association can
preserve membership, official notices, support requests, meeting summaries,
contribution responsibilities, member businesses, TrustSlips and evidence for
future partners or sponsors.

## What A Pilot Community Would Test

A pilot does not need to use every GSN product at once.

A sensible first pilot can test:

1. member identity and verification;
2. official notices and acknowledgement;
3. meeting or management pack;
4. promise/follow-up tracking;
5. one support/demand workflow;
6. one member-business or service visibility workflow;
7. basic records and evidence.

Success would mean:

- admins understand the tool without heavy explanation;
- members see a practical benefit;
- at least one real workflow improves compared with WhatsApp-only management;
- the organisation gives honest feedback;
- payment, sponsorship or contribution willingness can be discussed after value
  is seen.

## What GSN Is Not

GSN is not:

- a bank;
- a regulated lender;
- a government ID system;
- a replacement for safeguarding checks;
- a replacement for legal board minutes unless adopted by the organisation;
- a WhatsApp replacement;
- an automatic WhatsApp reader;
- a full CRM;
- a charity replacing the community;
- a guarantee that members will behave well;
- a way to expose private hardship publicly;
- an endorsement from Scottish Enterprise, Business Gateway or RGU.

## Pilot Participation Agreement

When a community is ready to begin a real pilot, the feature pack should be sent
with the short pilot agreement:

`docs/GSN_COMMUNITY_PILOT_PARTICIPATION_AGREEMENT_SHORT_DRAFT_2026-09-18.md`

Use the agreement to confirm:

- who the community or organisation contact is;
- that the pilot is a test, not a finished enterprise product;
- which available GSN features are included;
- what is covered by the monthly pilot fee;
- what extra support, training, integrations or third-party charges may cost
  more;
- the working pilot fee, currently drafted as GBP 25 per month unless changed in
  writing;
- the 30-day notice rule before ending the pilot or moving out of pilot pricing;
- the community's responsibilities around lawful use, member permission,
  administrator access and sensitive information;
- GSN's boundaries around banking, legal advice, safeguarding, guarantees and
  regulated services; and
- the signatures or written acceptance needed before the pilot starts.

The agreement should be completed with the date, community name, authorised
representative, payment day, agreed pilot scope and any agreed support fee before
it is sent for signature.

## Suggested Pilot Entry Offer

The simplest pilot offer:

GSN will help one organised community test whether digital community
infrastructure can improve official notices, meeting follow-up, member identity,
trusted requests, promise tracking, service visibility and evidence of value.

The first pilot should be small:

- admins and selected members first;
- one community space;
- one meeting or event workflow;
- one support/demand workflow;
- one commitment/follow-up workflow;
- one evidence summary after the pilot.

## Evidence We Want From Each Pilot

For each pilot, GSN should collect:

- what problem the community wanted solved;
- what tools they used before GSN;
- what GSN product they tested;
- what improved;
- what confused users;
- what still needs work;
- whether the organisation would pay, sponsor or recommend payment after a
  useful pilot;
- whether they would continue using the product.

## Final Boundary

This pack describes what GSN products can help a community do. It does not claim
that every product is fully complete, legally certified, regulated, funded,
endorsed or suitable for every organisation without configuration.

The honest pilot question is:

Can GSN help this community preserve and organise the value it already creates
better than scattered chat, paper, memory and isolated spreadsheets?
