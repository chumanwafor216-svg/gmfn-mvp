# GSN Advanced Analytics Opportunity Engine Brief - 2026-09-13

## Product decision

The full `Opportunity Engine` belongs under Analytics as a paid `Advanced Analytics` capability.

It should not be introduced as another Community Domain screen, a stream chat, a lightweight chatbot, a member conversation room, or a WhatsApp replacement. Community Domain remains one of the strongest contexts feeding the engine, but the product home should be Analytics because the engine analyses the total GSN operating field: business activity, marketplace movement, DemandBox patterns, community signals, trust evidence, relationship signals, person-to-person activity, and external environmental change.

Working label: `Opportunity Engine`.

Primary placement: `Analytics` -> `Advanced Analytics`.

Secondary feed: limited, periodic, plain-language snapshots may feed `Market Wisdom` as a fifth trail when explicitly scoped and approved. The full evidence, analysis, and suggested opportunity package stays in Advanced Analytics.

Community Domain role: governed data context, permissions boundary, and institutional lens. It is not the only UI home for the engine.

Owner value: help a business owner, community operator, market organiser, church, NGO, association, or local institution read what is changing around them and act earlier, using GSN's internal evidence plus cited external signals.

## Comparable market signals

Adjacent products already exist. This confirms the commercial direction is credible, but it also means GSN must differentiate clearly.

- Square Local Insights gives sellers local business trends and benchmarks, with Square AI adding charts and external context such as weather, events, news, and reviews.
- Realytics sells an AI business-advisor layer for local businesses, using competitor, block, customer, and market signals.
- ClearMarketReport produces local market, competitor, pricing, demographic, opportunity, risk, and short action-plan reports.
- Localook focuses on competitor intelligence, gap analysis, and local opportunities from public data.
- MuniMapz, LocationGeniusAI, and PinpointIQ show that geospatial and local-economy opportunity scoring is already a recognised category.
- Circle's community analytics shows that community engagement, member behaviour, and revenue analytics can be sold as a management layer.
- Horizon Scan AI and idea-discovery products show demand for strategic foresight and opportunity spotting.

The GSN gap is different: these tools generally do not combine governed community records, DemandBox requests, shop/Spotlight behaviour, TrustSlip and TrustPassport evidence, finance/support signals, and marketplace activity inside one community-first trust infrastructure.

## Core thesis

GSN should sell this as paid `Advanced Analytics`, not as generic `AI chat`.

A GSN environment already produces signals:

- what members ask for in DemandBox
- what shops publish, what people view, and what does not convert
- what support requests repeat
- what bulletins, programmes, and notices recur
- what trust and completion evidence accumulates
- what people ignore, delay, open, share, or respond to quickly
- what goods and services appear informally before they become businesses
- what finance, support, verification, or delivery friction keeps returning

The Opportunity Engine turns those signals into structured intelligence: facts first, evidence second, cautious interpretation third, suggested opportunities last.

## Total operating field

The Opportunity Engine should read the totality of GSN, not one feature in isolation.

It should be able to reason across:

- `Trust Graph` - who is connected, which relationships carry evidence, and where trust pathways are weak or strong
- `TrustPassport` - the fuller evidence story behind a person, community, shop, or institution
- `TrustSlip` - current, portable proof signals and what they make possible or risky
- `DemandBox` - what people are asking for, repeating, failing to find, or requesting from the community
- `Marketplace` - what is listed, viewed, ignored, contacted, fulfilled, repeated, or missing
- `Spotlight` - what is promoted, seen, opened, shared, and acted on
- `Shop Diary` - business media, product movement, owner effort, and customer response signals
- `Community Home` - active community context, governance posture, bulletins, membership movement, and next-action patterns
- `Community Domain` - the governed institutional setting, permissions, service configuration, and public/community operating rhythm
- `People interactions` - who the user is talking to, who is talking to the user, what requests or responses keep appearing, and what relationship evidence is forming
- `Place and environment` - the communities, local areas, markets, organisations, churches, schools, associations, or neighbourhoods where the user operates
- `External context` - outside public signals such as economy, policy, weather, events, local demand, local supply, funding, demographic shifts, and competitor movement, always cited where possible

The engine should connect what is happening inside GSN with what is happening outside the user, while keeping the analysis grounded in the same GSN community or operating environment. The goal is not to make broad generic predictions. The goal is to say: based on this user's GSN evidence, this community's activity, this local environment, and the wider outside signals, these are the opportunities, risks, and possible directions that appear to be forming.

The output should support different time horizons:

- now
- next 90 days
- next 1 year
- next 2 years
- next 5 years
- next 10 years

Every forecast must remain cautious. The engine may say `this may happen`, `this pattern may be forming`, or `this could be an opportunity`. It must not say the future is guaranteed.

## Placement model

### Free or standard surfaces

Market Wisdom may receive a small fifth trail:

- one short snapshot
- no private evidence exposure
- no deep source list
- no paid report detail
- no instruction that pretends to know the future
- a route into Advanced Analytics for deeper review

Shop Analytics may receive shop-level Opportunity Engine previews:

- product demand clues
- audience response gaps
- listing or pricing hypotheses
- repeat visitor/contact patterns
- suggested small experiments

Trust Analytics may receive evidence-readiness previews:

- missing trust evidence patterns
- weak verification coverage
- readiness for handover, sponsor proof, or institutional reporting

Community Domain analytics may receive governed domain-level previews:

- service gaps
- programme opportunities
- public bulletin response patterns
- member demand clusters
- sponsor or donor evidence readiness

### Paid surface

Advanced Analytics holds the full engine:

- evidence-backed opportunity reports
- PESTLE-style scans
- internal GSN signal summaries
- external cited context
- confidence and uncertainty
- risks and safeguards
- suggested experiments
- owner/admin review before publishing or acting

## First paid module shape

The first Advanced Analytics experience should use focused lanes:

- `Signals` - what changed recently across this business, shop, community, or domain
- `Market` - buying, selling, demand, pricing, product, and traffic movement
- `Economic` - affordability, jobs, spending, funding, supply, support, and revenue clues
- `Social` - household needs, community behaviour, education, faith, age groups, migration, wellbeing, and informal support patterns
- `Technology` - tools, automation, phone access, digital payments, AI, media, and operational improvement
- `Governance` - leadership, permissions, policy, institutional process, civic or organisational implications
- `Legal and Risk` - contracts, safeguarding, data, compliance, record duties, claims, and regulated-advice boundaries
- `Place and Environment` - weather, transport, housing condition, energy, waste, local environment, and resilience
- `Opportunities` - commercial or support opportunities GSN thinks may be emerging
- `Next Moves` - practical experiments framed as options, not instructions

## Output card contract

Each Advanced Analytics output card should include:

- `Signal` - what GSN observed
- `Evidence` - which internal records or external sources support the observation
- `Interpretation` - what it may mean, using cautious language
- `Opportunity` - what could be built, sold, organised, tested, or funded
- `Risk` - what could go wrong or be misunderstood
- `Time horizon` - now, 90 days, 1 year, 5 years, or 10 years
- `Confidence` - low, medium, or high
- `Suggested next step` - one practical action the owner may choose
- `Human review` - owner/admin confirms before publishing or acting

## Data boundary

The engine must use structured GSN records first. External information must be cited and treated as context, not secret knowledge. AI suggestions must not pretend to be legal advice, medical advice, financial advice, political instruction, guarantee, diagnosis, proof of demand, or proof of future success.

The engine must not become surveillance. It should analyse patterns, records, and operating signals. It must avoid hidden personal scoring, public shaming, identity exposure, or cross-community leakage.

## Paid-product boundary

The paid product can package:

- monthly opportunity brief
- weekly market and community signal scan
- donor/sponsor readiness brief
- shop/service opportunity report
- programme design suggestions
- early warning report
- market gap report
- community needs evidence pack
- launch experiment plan
- CAC/LTV and conversion interpretation when enough data exists

## Notification workstream boundary

Instant phone notifications, vibration, sound, and real-time alerts are important, but they are a separate second-priority workstream.

The product should not promise WhatsApp-like buzzing until Web Push, service worker registration, browser permissions, mobile platform limits, PWA install state, notification routing, and user consent are verified. Notifications should support the Opportunity Engine later, but they are not the Opportunity Engine itself.

## Devil-truth boundary

This feature can become more valuable than ordinary shop listings if it reliably helps owners see opportunities earlier. It can also become dangerous if it hallucinates, exposes private records, gives regulated advice, or turns weak activity signals into false certainty.

The UI must present opportunity intelligence as evidence-led suggestions requiring human judgment, not as prophecy, instruction, guarantee, or authority.
