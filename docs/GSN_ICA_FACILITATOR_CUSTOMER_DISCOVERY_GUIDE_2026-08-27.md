# GSN ICA Facilitator Customer Discovery Guide

Prepared for: Igbo Cultural Association in Aberdeen (ICA)
Prepared by: Chuma Nwafor
Date: 2026-08-27
Purpose: prepare the founder to run a 15-20 minute customer discovery conversation before sharing the separate GSN participant paper.

---

## 1. Your Real Objective

You are not going to ICA to beg, impress, lecture, or prove that GSN is clever.

You are going to test whether ICA already has valuable community assets that are
not being organised well enough:

- trust;
- identity;
- relationships;
- contribution;
- member knowledge;
- welfare practices;
- business skills;
- job and referral networks;
- leadership memory;
- informal guarantees;
- community reputation;
- opportunity flow.

The core question is:

```text
Can GSN help ICA turn existing community value into stronger administration,
stronger member support, stronger economic opportunity, and stronger community
memory without changing the identity of the association?
```

For this session, interrogate four possible ICA stories. ICA is the worked
example, but listen for language that would also fit churches, schools,
professional bodies, alumni groups, NGOs, welfare groups, and project
committees:

```text
1. Administrative pain: meetings, notices, responses, decisions, attendance,
   handover, and records.
2. Verification pain: member standing, references, movement from Aberdeen,
   welfare/support proof, business trust, and committee confirmation.
3. Economic opportunity pain: member businesses, Demand Box needs, referrals,
   jobs, services, grants, contracts, and trusted commerce.
4. Cultural continuity and community memory pain: events, service, identity,
   leadership handover, grant evidence, caseworker reading, and future members.
```

Do not try to make all four win. The room must reveal which one is painful
enough to test first.

GSN should not be framed as an outside saviour. It should be framed as a tool
that helps ICA capitalise on what ICA already has.

---

## 2. The Psychological Frame

ICA members are intelligent adults. Many will have work experience, education,
families, obligations, business ambition, community pride, and scepticism.

Do not speak to them as poor people needing rescue.

Speak to them as a serious diaspora association carrying untapped economic and
social value.

Use this mental frame:

```text
We are not poor in community.
We are poor in organised community infrastructure.
```

Say it more softly in the room:

```text
Many diaspora communities already have trust, contribution, discipline, and
relationship value. The problem is that much of that value stays scattered in
memory, WhatsApp, private conversations, and leadership burden. GSN is exploring
whether that value can be organised into support, proof, and opportunity.
```

---

## 3. The Hard Truth About The Room

Many people will support an idea politely if it sounds cultural, community-based,
or created by someone they know.

That is not enough.

Your job is to find out:

- what ICA actually struggles with;
- what members will actually use;
- what executives will actually administer;
- what economic or support benefit they can see for themselves;
- what privacy or misuse risk could make them reject it;
- whether a small follow-up is deserved.

If they do not see a practical benefit, they will not adopt GSN just because it
sounds noble.

Unabated truth:

```text
If people do not see their own gain, their own protection, or their own reduced
stress, they will not keep using it.
```

---

## 4. Your 15-20 Minute Structure

Keep the first 10-15 minutes as customer discovery.

Use the last 5 minutes to point them toward the four ICA stories, the three GSN engines, and the share
paper.

### 20-Minute Version

```text
0-2 minutes: Respect, context, and honesty rule
2-6 minutes: How ICA works today
6-10 minutes: One painful ICA decision or operational gap
10-13 minutes: Trust, privacy, authority, and refusal risk
13-18 minutes: Three GSN engines and product-fit questions
18-20 minutes: Close and next step
```

### 15-Minute Version

```text
0-2 minutes: Context and honesty rule
2-6 minutes: Current ICA trust/support/opportunity process
6-9 minutes: One practical decision or problem
9-10 minutes: Privacy or refusal risk
10-15 minutes: Three GSN engines and close
```

Time rule:

```text
Do not let yourself explain GSN before ICA has explained ICA.
```

---

## 5. Opening Script

Use this:

```text
Thank you for giving me this time.

I am part of this community, so I know ICA is not just a social group. It is a
place of identity, support, relationships, culture, family connection,
responsibility, and opportunity.

I am not here to sell another WhatsApp group. I am trying to understand whether
GSN can help ICA organise the value it already has: member support, official
notices, contribution, trust, referrals, business visibility, and opportunities.

For the first 10-15 minutes, I want to ask about ICA. In the last 5 minutes, I
will explain GSN briefly and ask whether it fits anything you have said.

Please be honest. If it is not useful, say so. Criticism will help me more than
polite encouragement.
```

Then stop talking and ask the first question.

---

## 6. Customer Discovery Questions

Ask these in order. Do not ask all follow-ups if time is short.

### Question 1: Current Reality

```text
How does ICA currently keep important association matters organised: notices,
members, dues, welfare, opportunities, volunteers, and decisions?
```

Listen for:

- WhatsApp overload;
- officer memory;
- manual chasing;
- buried announcements;
- unclear member records;
- informal support requests;
- missed opportunities;
- contribution confusion;
- leadership burden.

### Question 2: Community Value

```text
What valuable things already exist inside ICA that are not being fully used?
```

Prompt if needed:

- member skills;
- businesses;
- professional contacts;
- job referrals;
- cultural knowledge;
- trusted helpers;
- welfare support;
- event participation;
- elder/community wisdom;
- young member talent;
- links to churches, workplaces, family associations, and other UK communities.

### Question 3: Economic Strength

```text
How could ICA become more economically useful to members without losing its
cultural and community identity?
```

Do not make this abstract. Ask for practical examples:

- jobs;
- contracts;
- grants;
- referrals;
- member businesses;
- emergency support;
- savings discipline;
- welfare funds;
- professional introductions;
- trusted services.

### Question 4: One Painful Decision

```text
If ICA could improve only one process first, which one would make the biggest
difference?
```

Then classify it:

```text
Is this mainly administrative pain, verification pain, economic opportunity
pain, or community memory pain?
```

Possible answers:

- official notices not getting buried;
- meeting setup, response chasing, attendance, summaries, or decisions;
- dues and contribution tracking;
- welfare or emergency support request;
- trusted referrals;
- member verification or reference for someone moving from Aberdeen;
- member business visibility;
- volunteer coordination;
- opportunity sharing;
- leadership handover;
- cultural event memory and grant/caseworker summary evidence;
- verification of members and helpers.

Force one priority. Do not accept "all of them".

When they choose one, translate it into the same structure used in the take-home
paper: what the workflow contains, what records GSN would keep, what can be
counted in a weekly/monthly report, what belongs in Governance, and what must
not be overclaimed.

### Question 5: Trust And Authority

```text
For that one process, who should be allowed to confirm, approve, or verify the
record?
```

Listen for:

- president;
- secretary;
- financial secretary;
- welfare officer;
- executive committee;
- elders;
- event organisers;
- ordinary members;
- guarantors or sponsors;
- outside partners.

### Question 6: Privacy And Refusal

```text
What would make ICA reject a system like this?
```

Prompt if needed:

- too much exposure;
- gossip;
- politics;
- public shame;
- executive overreach;
- wrong records;
- confusing technology;
- no visible benefit;
- asking older members to do too much;
- looking like a bank, credit-score app, or surveillance tool.

### Question 7: Real Adoption Pressure Test

Ask this if there is time, or use it as your final truth question:

```text
If this existed today, what would make an ICA member actually open it again next week?
```

Then ask:

```text
What would make an executive actually use it instead of returning to WhatsApp and memory?
```

Listen for practical value, not compliments:

- saves executive time;
- keeps notices visible;
- helps track responses;
- makes dues clearer;
- routes welfare support better;
- exposes member businesses;
- protects sensitive matters;
- creates visible opportunity;
- gives each member personal benefit.

If nobody can answer this, adoption is weak.

## 7. Last 5 Minutes: Product Discovery Frame

Only after they answer the discovery questions, introduce GSN through three
engines.

Say:

```text
Based on what you have said, GSN has three engines that may matter for ICA.
```

### Engine 1: Community Value Maintenance

```text
This engine helps ICA keep important community value from disappearing.
It can support official notices, member responses, dues purpose, support
requests, event contribution, member skills, businesses, and association memory.
```

Plain meaning:

```text
What ICA already does should not be buried in chat or trapped in one person's
memory.
```

### Engine 2: Verification And Trust Transport

```text
This engine helps verified evidence move safely from one setting to another.
A member's service, contribution, reliability, business activity, or support
history can be confirmed by the right authority and shared only when appropriate.
```

Plain meaning:

```text
Trust should not be reduced to gossip or popularity. It should have context,
confirmation, privacy, and human judgement.
```

### Engine 3: Opportunity Engine

```text
This engine helps ICA turn community trust into practical opportunity: jobs,
referrals, member businesses, grants, trusted services, support pathways,
contracts, partnerships, and stronger community visibility.
```

Plain meaning:

```text
The point is not just to record activity. The point is to help members become
stronger economically and socially.
```

Then ask:

```text
Which of these three engines feels most useful for ICA first?
```

Do not argue with the answer.

## 8. Executive And Member Value Check

Before you close, make sure both sides can see themselves in GSN.

Ask executives:

```text
Which part of your day-to-day ICA work currently takes too much chasing,
remembering, explaining, or manual follow-up?
```

Prompt only if needed:

- posting official notices;
- collecting meeting responses;
- dues and contribution follow-up;
- welfare/support routing;
- opportunity sharing;
- volunteer coordination;
- member business visibility;
- leadership handover;
- records for accountability.

Ask ordinary members:

```text
What would make GSN personally useful enough for you to open it without being
reminded by the executives?
```

Prompt only if needed:

- seeing official notices clearly;
- finding jobs or referrals;
- finding trusted ICA businesses;
- seeing contribution history;
- requesting support privately;
- knowing what opportunities are active;
- being recognised for service or contribution;
- carrying verified trust evidence beyond ICA.

Truth rule:

```text
If executives see admin value but members see no personal value, adoption will
be weak. If members see value but executives see extra work, adoption will also
be weak. GSN must reduce work for leaders and create visible gain for members.
```

## 9. Immediate Signup Without Pressure

The close should invite action without making the room feel trapped.

Say:

```text
If this sounds useful, I am not asking ICA to adopt everything today. I am asking
for a small honest test. Those who want to help can join the GSN app as pilot
testers, choose the ICA context, and tell us what is useful, confusing, risky,
or missing.
```

Ask:

```text
Who would be willing to join the app today only as a tester, not as a final
adopter?
```

Then separate the signals:

```text
[ ] wants to test as executive/admin
[ ] wants to test as ordinary member
[ ] wants to test member business/opportunity flow
[ ] wants to test welfare/support flow
[ ] wants to observe but not sign up yet
[ ] refuses for now
```

Unabated truth:

```text
A small number of honest testers is better than a room full of polite people who
never open the app again.
```

## 10. Your Strongest One-Minute Explanation

If you only get one minute, say this:

```text
ICA already has value: trust, members, culture, contribution, businesses,
skills, welfare, and relationships. But much of that value is scattered across
WhatsApp, memory, and informal processes.

GSN is not trying to change ICA. It is trying to help ICA organise what already
exists so the association can support members better, protect trust, preserve
records, and open more opportunities.

I want to know whether this is genuinely useful for ICA, and if yes, which one
small workflow should be tested first.
```

---

## 11. What Not To Say

Do not say:

- Africans are poor and need saving.
- ICA is disorganised.
- GSN will make members rich.
- GSN will guarantee trust.
- GSN replaces WhatsApp.
- GSN replaces executives.
- GSN will solve every problem.
- GSN can silently track attendance by Bluetooth.
- QR or Bluetooth presence proves contribution or trust.
- ICA has endorsed GSN.
- Pressure everyone to sign up today.

Better language:

```text
Diaspora communities already create value. The question is whether we can
organise that value into support, trust, and opportunity.
```

```text
WhatsApp is good for conversation. GSN is for the things that need structure,
visibility, records, and follow-through.
```

```text
The first test should be small. One notice, one response flow, one support or
opportunity workflow, then honest feedback. If the value is clear, invite
people to join the app immediately as pilot testers, not as forced adopters.
```

---

## 12. Signs You Are Winning

You are winning if they say things like:

- "Yes, important notices get buried."
- "The financial secretary needs something like this."
- "Members should see opportunities before they disappear."
- "Support requests need privacy."
- "Only executives should approve that."
- "Businesses inside ICA should be more visible."
- "This could help new members know where to go."
- "Can we test it with one announcement or one dues process?"

You are not winning if they only say:

- "This is nice."
- "Well done."
- "This is a good idea."
- "We will think about it."

Polite approval is weak. Specific operational interest is strong.

## 14. Close Script

Use this:

```text
Thank you. I do not want to pretend this is validated because we had one good
conversation.

The real question is whether ICA has one workflow worth testing: official
notices, dues, welfare support, member businesses, opportunities, or verified
contribution.

If there is interest, the next step should be small: one workflow, a few people,
clear privacy rules, and honest feedback.
```

Then hand them or send them the participant paper.
