## 1. Purpose

```text
Run a focused 15-20 minute discovery conversation with Igbo Cultural
Association in Aberdeen to learn how the association currently builds trust,
remembers contribution, handles responsibility, and decides what evidence is
worth preserving.
```

This is not a product launch.
This is not an ICA endorsement request.
This is not a full workshop.
This is not a feature tour.

The session has one job:

```text
Find one real ICA decision where better community memory or trusted evidence
could help, then spend the final 5 minutes testing whether GSN sounds like a
credible way to support that decision.
```

Unabated truth: if the first 10-15 minutes are spent explaining GSN, the session
has already gone wrong. Ask about ICA first. Product discovery comes last.

---

## 2. The 20-Minute Rule

Maximum session length: 20 minutes.

Preferred length: 15 minutes.

Product discovery window: final 5 minutes only.

```text
0-2 minutes: Set context, consent, and honesty rule
2-6 minutes: Current ICA trust and community-memory process
6-10 minutes: One real decision where evidence matters
10-13 minutes: Privacy, authority, and refusal point
13-18 minutes: Product discovery: explain GSN briefly and test fit
18-20 minutes: Close, score signal, and agree next step if warranted
```

If only 15 minutes are available:

```text
0-2 minutes: Context and consent
2-6 minutes: Current ICA trust process
6-9 minutes: One real decision and who confirms it
9-10 minutes: Privacy or refusal point
10-15 minutes: Product discovery and close
```

Hard time rule:

```text
At minute 13 in a 20-minute session, move to product discovery.
At minute 10 in a 15-minute session, move to product discovery.
```

## 3. Opening Script

Read this plainly:

```text
Thank you for making time.

I want to be clear: this is not a product launch, not a sales meeting, and not
an endorsement request from ICA.

I am trying to understand how Igbo Cultural Association in Aberdeen currently
builds trust, remembers contribution, supports members, and makes decisions.

For the first part, I do not want to talk much about the app. I want to learn
how ICA works today. In the final 5 minutes, I will explain GSN briefly and ask
whether it fits any real problem you have named.

Please be direct. Criticism is more useful than politeness. Please do not share
private allegations or sensitive details about another named person.
```

Then ask immediately:

```text
When ICA needs to know who is active, reliable, helpful, responsible, or safe to
recommend, how does that currently happen?
```

## 4. Minute-By-Minute Facilitator Guide

### 0-2 Minutes: Context And Consent

Goal: make the room safe enough to disagree.

Say:

```text
This is discovery, so the best outcome is honest learning. If GSN is solving
the wrong problem, I need to know that quickly.
```

Do not open with a deck. Do not open with the app.

### 2-6 Minutes: Current ICA Trust Process

Ask:

```text
How does ICA currently know who contributes, helps, attends, volunteers, keeps
promises, or can be trusted with responsibility?
```

Fast follow-ups:

- Who usually knows this information?
- Is it written anywhere or mostly remembered?
- Does it live in WhatsApp, officers' memory, event records, contribution lists,
  or personal relationships?
- What gets lost when leaders change or members become less active?

Listen for the real operating system ICA already has.

### 6-10 Minutes: One Real Decision

Ask:

```text
What is one decision ICA makes where better evidence or memory would help?
```

Prompt only if they get stuck:

- confirming active membership;
- choosing volunteers or organisers;
- recommending a member or member business;
- coordinating welfare or support;
- tracking contribution or repayment behaviour;
- knowing who attended, served, helped, or followed through;
- passing knowledge from old leaders to new leaders.

Force one answer:

```text
If you could fix only one of these first, which one matters most for ICA?
```

Do not accept "all of them" as discovery evidence.

### 10-13 Minutes: Privacy, Authority, And Refusal

Ask three fast questions:

```text
Who should be allowed to confirm that this evidence is true?
```

```text
What should never be public or visible to ordinary members?
```

```text
What would make ICA refuse to use a system like this?
```

This is the most important non-product section. The product is dangerous if the
authority and privacy model is weak.

### 13-18 Minutes: Product Discovery

Now, and only now, explain GSN:

```text
GSN is a community trust infrastructure tool.

It does not create trust for ICA and it should not score people. It tries to
help ICA preserve selected evidence of membership, contribution, service,
commerce, and responsibility, with clear confirmation, privacy, and correction
rules.

The goal is to support human decisions, not replace them.
```

Then ask:

```text
Based on the ICA decision you named, does GSN sound useful, unnecessary,
risky, or unclear?
```

Follow-up:

```text
If GSN supported only that one decision first, what would it need to show, and
what must it hide?
```

If showing the app, show only one narrow path:

```text
Community Home -> Trust Events -> Trust Passport -> TrustSlip -> Public check
```

Do not show admin tools, finance tools, marketplace breadth, settings, or long
dashboards. In a 15-20 minute session, showing too much product will pollute the
discovery evidence.

### 18-20 Minutes: Close

Ask:

```text
What is the one thing I should fix or clarify before speaking to another ICA
member or organiser?
```

Then ask:

```text
Would ICA be open to a longer follow-up only if this one decision is worth
exploring properly?
```

Record:

```text
[ ] yes
[ ] maybe
[ ] no
Condition:
```

## 5. The Five Questions To Print

If you print only one page, print this.

```text
1. How does ICA currently know who is active, reliable, helpful, responsible, or
   safe to recommend?

2. Where does that evidence live today: memory, WhatsApp, officer records,
   contribution lists, event attendance, or personal relationships?

3. What is one real ICA decision where better evidence or community memory would
   help?

4. Who should be allowed to confirm that evidence, and what should stay private?

5. In the final 5 minutes only: does GSN sound useful, unnecessary, risky, or
   unclear for that one decision?
```

## 6. ICA-Specific Hypotheses To Test

These are hypotheses, not claims.

```text
Hypothesis 1:
ICA may have useful trust knowledge spread across leaders, members, WhatsApp,
attendance, contribution records, and personal memory.

Hypothesis 2:
ICA may need better community memory when leaders change, members become
inactive, responsibilities rotate, or recommendations are made.

Hypothesis 3:
ICA may value evidence around membership, contribution, volunteering,
responsibility, welfare support, trusted referrals, or member business activity.

Hypothesis 4:
ICA may reject GSN if it feels like surveillance, gossip, ranking, public shame,
leader overreach, or unnecessary bureaucracy.
```

The session should try to destroy these hypotheses, not defend them.

---

## 7. What To Listen For

Strong signal:

- They name a real decision before you suggest many examples.
- They explain where ICA trust evidence currently lives.
- They can say who should confirm evidence.
- They name what must stay private.
- They give a concrete refusal reason.
- They ask what a narrow follow-up would look like.

Weak signal:

- They only say "this is interesting".
- They mostly react to the app interface.
- They cannot name a decision.
- They do not care who confirms records.
- They do not see any privacy or misuse risk.

Fail signal:

- They read GSN as social scoring.
- They think WhatsApp and memory are enough for every relevant ICA decision.
- They see recording trust evidence as more harmful than useful.
- They cannot identify any first workflow that would justify a follow-up.

## 8. Product Discovery Boundary

Use the last 5 minutes to test fit, not to sell.

Say:

```text
Based only on what you have told me about ICA, I want to test whether GSN fits
or whether it is the wrong tool.
```

Ask:

```text
Would this help the decision you named, or would it create more work than value?
```

```text
What would the first screen need to make clear for an ICA organiser or member?
```

```text
What word, feature, or behaviour would make you distrust the product?
```

Do not ask:

- Do you like GSN?
- Would everyone use this?
- Is this a good business?
- Can I say ICA supports this?
- Should we add more features?

## 14. Stop Rule

Do not expand this into a full workshop before ICA proves the problem is real.

Next proof:

```text
1. Run one 15-20 minute ICA discovery conversation.
2. Write the memo within 24 hours.
3. Run 2-3 more short conversations if the signal is unclear.
4. Only then propose a longer follow-up or pilot workflow.
```

If the session produces only compliments, the evidence is weak.

If it produces one painful ICA decision, one evidence source, one confirmation
rule, one privacy boundary, one refusal risk, and one follow-up condition, it is
useful.
